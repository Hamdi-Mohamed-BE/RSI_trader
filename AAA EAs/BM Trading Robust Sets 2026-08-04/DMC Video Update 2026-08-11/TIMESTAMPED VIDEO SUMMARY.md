## 00:00–00:57 — Purpose

The presenter argues that traders often fail because they keep changing strategies, consume contradictory information, or lack a complete plan. He introduces “Dumb Money Concepts” (DMC) as a deliberately simplified alternative to Smart Money Concepts. The stated goal is to use a small number of repeatable rules so the trader can focus on execution and discipline.

## 00:57–01:47 — Core price pattern

The core question is whether price gains a level, loses it, or attempts to cross it and fails. The basic reversal pattern is:

1. Price reaches an important level.
2. A wick or other attempt passes through the level.
3. The candle closes back inside the prior range.
4. Price is expected to reverse toward the origin of the move or the opposite side of the zone.

The presenter stresses that this setup only claims to identify the next likely move, not every later continuation.

## 01:47–03:36 — Selecting levels

The process starts on higher timeframes. Monthly and weekly candle-body levels that have not yet been revisited are treated as the most important potential pivot points. Daily levels and recently passed-through levels can also be marked.

The first test of a previously untouched level is considered stronger. Once a level has already produced a reaction, the presenter considers it less likely to hold on another test unless a fresh level formed at the same location. When a used level fails, price may continue to the next level behind it.

## 03:36–05:08 — Direction and “trend”

Trend is not defined through a standard higher-high/higher-low rule. Instead, the presenter defines direction as the next anticipated higher-timeframe move. A failure at a weekly or monthly level supplies a broad target and direction; intraday trades should preferably align with that move.

Lower-timeframe direction can change before higher-timeframe direction because the structures are treated as fractal and cascading. The presenter therefore prioritizes the expected move between higher-timeframe levels over a conventional moving-average or market-structure trend label.

## 05:08–06:05 — Regaining a level

A wick rejection and a rapid regain are treated as related information. If price closes beyond a level but quickly closes back inside the zone, that regain is additional confirmation that price may travel toward the zone’s opposite side. A small retest of the regained level can precede the move.

## 06:05–07:16 — Entry alternatives

Three entry styles are presented:

- Blind entry at a previously untouched daily, weekly, or monthly level.
- Confirmation entry after price reacts at the level.
- Limited dollar-cost averaging across a predefined higher-timeframe zone.

Blind entries may obtain the best price but can experience substantial drawdown when the level does not hold. The presenter describes them as more suitable for strong higher-timeframe levels and relatively quick targets.

## 07:16–08:03 — Confirmation and retest

For a lower-timeframe level, the presenter prefers observing a rejection wick and then entering on a retest of that same level. The stop is placed beyond invalidation and the target is the other side or next important level.

The video explicitly does not supply every numerical stop, target, or nuance. The presenter says those details are demonstrated elsewhere. Therefore, the public video alone does not define a fully deterministic strategy capable of reproducing the advertised hit rate.

## 08:03–09:14 — DCA example and restriction

A Bitcoin example illustrates splitting an intended entry zone into a small number of purchases. If price later returns to a former support/resistance area, the weaker entry can be removed around break-even, leaving the better-priced entry. The presenter warns against continuing to add indefinitely and distinguishes the idea from martingale.

## 09:14–10:03 — Profit-taking

Suggested exits include:

- the next important key level;
- the next swing high or swing low;
- scaling out in a way similar to the staged entry.

Entry and target timeframe should be consistent. A weekly-level entry should not automatically be exited at an insignificant daily fluctuation if the original higher-timeframe objective remains valid.

## 10:03–10:38 — Exit when the expected move changes

If a position fails to make the anticipated new high or low and price behavior implies that the next move has changed direction, the presenter recommends exiting rather than becoming attached to the trade. Re-entry later is acceptable if the original setup returns.

## 10:38–11:28 — Risk, stop placement and martingale warning

The risk discussion is subjective: size should be meaningful enough to expose emotional mistakes but not so large that the trader must constantly monitor the position. No percentage formula is provided.

Although the presenter says he does not use stops frequently, his protective-stop guidance is to place the stop beyond the next structural level behind the entry level. This is intended to survive a sweep of the obvious level. He closes with a warning not to use martingale and limits any DCA idea to one or two additional entries.
