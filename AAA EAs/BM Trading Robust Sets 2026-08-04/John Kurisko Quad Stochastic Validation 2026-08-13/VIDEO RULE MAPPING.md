# Video rule mapping — John Kurisko quad-stochastic method

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PpysVy2NNQ4

The timestamps below refer to the English audio in the linked interview.

## Exact rules stated in the video

- **Chart:** one-minute bars (08:16–08:30).
- **Fast stochastic parameters:** 9-3-1, 14-3-1, 44-1-1, and 60-10-1 (13:43–14:04).
- **Role of the indicators:** 9-3 is the one-minute timing oscillator; 60-10 approximates the larger five-minute momentum context (15:08–16:18 and 41:45–42:00).
- **Quad rotation:** all four stochastic readings are below 20 for a long environment, or symmetrically above 80 for a short environment (17:04–18:24).
- **Quad rotation alone is not an entry:** it is only half of the “super signal” (18:17–18:29).
- **Reversal confirmation:** price bounces and retests the prior low/high while the stochastic makes a higher low/lower high. Buy/sell on the renewed turn (18:29–18:49).
- **Stop:** beyond the retest candle lows/highs (18:49–18:56 and 28:52–29:18).
- **Divergence price comparison:** the speaker later says candle bodies matter more than tails for identifying the double bottom/top, although the protective stop goes beyond the wick (28:29–29:18).
- **Exit:** close a long when the 9-3 reaches 80; close a short at the symmetric 20 level (21:24–21:51 and 62:00–62:59).
- **Continuation/flag long:** 60-10 remains embedded above roughly 85, 9-3 pulls back toward 20, and price is above the 20-period moving average. The short is symmetric (42:00–45:47).
- **Trend aging:** favor the first two flags in an embedded trend, then become cautious (45:00–45:34).
- **Context filters:** understand the active channel and broader pressure; avoid major news such as FOMC and jobs reports (19:02–20:18 and 65:18–65:27).

## Claims that are not accepted as validation

- The speaker estimates that about eight of ten properly selected setups should initially move into profit, then later mentions an 85–90% result for perfect execution. These are personal estimates, not independently audited statistics (19:29–19:38 and 23:35–23:41).
- A platform display shows 156 trades and 98% profitable on a Bitcoin chart, but the speaker immediately says he does not know what it is counting, does not believe that display, and had ES parameters applied to Bitcoin (25:30–26:11).
- The speaker explicitly says he does not believe in mechanical backtesting because channel and market-pulse judgment are part of his process (18:57–19:29).

## Mechanical assumptions required for this validation

The video does not completely specify pivot detection, tolerance for an “equal” body low/high, stop padding, maximum holding time, exact channel construction, or a machine-readable news calendar. The validation therefore uses only past-confirmed oscillator pivots, body retests measured in ATR, a wick-based stop with ATR padding, a time stop, and the stated 20-period moving-average filter for continuation trades.

The discretionary one-two-three channel is deliberately not reconstructed with future pivots. This prevents look-ahead bias but means the program tests the video’s indicator edge, not the presenter’s full discretionary skill.
