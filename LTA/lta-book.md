## PoC (Point of Control) | Standard 

The Point of Control is the price level within the volume profile where the highest amount of trading activity or volume has occurred. It’s essentially the peak of the volume distribution curve. This level signifies the price point that has attracted the most buying and selling interest from market participants within the analyzed range and time period. 

## VaH (Value Area High) | Standard 

This refers to the highest price level within the value area. The value area is typically defined as the price range where the majority of trading volume has occurred. In other words, VaH represents the upper boundary of this range, beyond which trading volume decreases significantly. 

## VaL (Value Area Low) | Standard 

Conversely, the Value Area Low represents the lowest price level within the value area. It is the lower boundary of the price range where most trading volume has occurred. Similar to VaH, VaL indicates a point where trading volume starts to drop off significantly below this level.

## Low Volume Node

A Low Volume Node can indicate a point of equilibrium or indifference in the market, where neither buyers nor sellers have shown strong interest. Traders often view these areas as potential areas of interest for future price movements.

Just as traders gathered at the opening of the week to set the tone for market activity, so too did citizens convene at the Forum to engage in commerce and exchange goods in ancient Rome.

## Fixed Ranges are essentialwhen there's a clearly defined area of consolidation.

As such, it's vital to closely monitor our key levels, such as the Fixed PoC (Fixed Point of Control), Fixed VaH (Fixed Value Area High), and Fixed VaL (Fixed Value Area Low). ## Consolidation: 

This is where the market is considered to be gathering energy, with buyers and sellers in rough equilibrium. The volume profile will show the majority of trading activity occurred at these levels, indicating a significant area of value. 

## Expansion: 

After the consolidation phase, there is a sharp up or downward movement in price, which is labeled as expansion. This indicates a breakout where the price moves significantly in one direction, showing that buyers or sellers have taken control and are pushing the price higher or lower. 

## Retracement: 

Following the expansion, the price begins to fall back down. This is labeled as a retracement, which is a common phenomenon where prices temporarily reverse direction after a strong trend. This can be due to traders taking profits or a reduction in the momentum that drove the initial expansion. 

## Continuation: 

After the retracement, the price movement starts trending upwards again. This is labeled as continuation, indicating that the prior uptrend is resuming.
